Follow the wire for the build

01 · The electronics

Scanner electronics: two Nanos and a radio link

The scan above is a simulation of how it works. This is how the real prototype is wired, and the photos are the boards I built.

Pin numbers shown are the ones given in the paper, or fixed by the Nano's SPI bus.

Sensor

TF Mini LiDAR

Measures range. It talks 3.3 V serial, so the line from the 5 V Nano into it goes through a resistor divider.

Motion

Two 9 g servos

Pan and tilt the sensor on a 3D-printed mount.

Controller

Arduino Nano

Steps the servos and reads the sensor at each angle.

Radio

nRF24L01 link

On the Nano's SPI bus with chip-select, enable and interrupt on D10, D9 and D8. Sends each angle and range.

Ground station

Nano on Veroboard

A second Nano and radio, hand-wired, feeding the laptop over USB.

Software

Processing sketch

Plots every point as the scan runs.

My part

I designed the mount, built the scanner and its ground station, wrote the plotting code and ran the tests. The paper is co-authored with my mentor, Shekhar Jain.

02 · Real scans

Scanner tests in rooms and over a water tub

Point clouds from the prototype. Tested in a lit room, a dark room and daylight, then over a tub of water with sand and blocks on the bottom. A full scan takes about ten minutes.

03 · What went wrong

Two scanner problems the tests exposed

  • The first mount was bulky

    It was built round full-size metal-gear servos. I redesigned it for 9 g micro servos.

  • Room light added noise to the scans

    Scans were cleaner in low light and with the sensor close to the water surface.
