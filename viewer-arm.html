One socket, a tool for each task

A body-powered hook does one job. This arm keeps the forearm and swaps the hand: each attachment is shaped for one activity and changes over in seconds, without help.

    Why this design

    Five first ideas scored against the specification, out of 20. Hover a bar for the breakdown.

    Scores by specification

    An arm amputee who reviewed the five ideas ranked the activity-specific design first, because it met the most of the specification.

    Twist on, pin with the key

    Changing an attachment takes one hand: a short turn and a key. Drag the Attach slider or press Play, and use Section cut to see what holds it.

    Turn still to go
    °
    State
    1. Line upHold the attachment an eighth of a turn off, so its four slots sit over the four lugs on the wrist end.
    2. LowerThe slots pass over the lugs and the collar seats on the socket.
    3. Turn 45° clockwiseThe lugs travel round a channel inside the collar and end up under solid plastic, so the attachment cannot lift off.
    4. Push in the keyThe key goes through the collar wall into a lug and stops the collar turning back.

    To remove: pull the key, turn anticlockwise, lift.

    The four lugs, the slotted collar and the key are visible on the print. The channel inside the collar is drawn from how those parts must fit; it was not measured.

    Five sizes from one chart

    Printing makes each socket to order. Pick a size and the socket rescales to that row of the sizing chart. The twist-lock stays the same, so every attachment fits every arm.
