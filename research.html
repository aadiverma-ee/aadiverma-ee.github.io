Follow the wire for the build

01 · The boards

Two interface boards for two kinds of solar cell

Laid out in KiCad and checked on paper at full size against the real cell before ordering. The cell is driven forward, so in each circuit it is drawn as a light-emitting diode.

71 × 50 mmLarge-cell board, two layers
27 × 25.5 mmSmall board, two cells in parallel
1.5 mmPower traces between pads

Circuit 1

Large-cell board

Three front pads take the cell's tabs: positive in the middle, return on each side. One via for each net carries the current through to the wire pads on the back.

Circuit 2

Small-cell board

Two small cell modules wired in parallel. One via for each net leads to a wire pad on the back, where the adapter leads are soldered.

About 3.2 AWhat a 1.5 mm track carries for a 10 °C rise, by IPC-2221
0.5 ABench supply limit, about a sixth of that
About 18 mΩResistance round the large board

The current and resistance figures are estimates: 1 oz copper assumed, track lengths taken from the KiCad renders.

02 · The housing

A cell housing that screws into a light socket

A square-to-round shell, 2 mm thick, holds the board flush and sits on a standard E26 lamp adapter. That is how the cell mounts in the sphere above.

  • First print was too tight

    Measured it against the board, opened the pocket and reprinted. The second print fitted.

  • A board off by 1 mm misses the contacts

    Printed the layout on paper at full size and laid it on the real cell before ordering.

My part

I owned the hardware on the solar-cell side: the boards and housings that hold the cells, the light source for early link tests and the bench setup. The receiver and modulator boards are the lab's designs.

03 · Test transmitter

A stage light made into a data transmitter

The lab needed a light source for link tests. I took a 14-channel DMX stage light with no manual and put it under Python control.

04 · Result

Flash-rate jitter cut from 24% to 2%

Python's sleep() fell behind above 60 Hz. Busy-wait timing tracks the command to 100 Hz: 40 Hz measured 41.0, 60 measured 60.3, 100 measured 100.5.

05 · Result

Six bytes of data read back off the light

A photodiode recorded 18 s of flashing. Each burst is one byte, and each bit is a quarter second of fast or slow flashes.

  • Phone video aliased the strobe

    It read 11, 4 and 3.6 Hz for one setting, so I moved to a photodiode on the oscilloscope.

  • The LED's own 1.9 kHz dimming hid the flashes

    Filtered it out before measuring flash rate.

  • First capture was shorter than one bit

    Recorded 18 s, not 0.2 s, and decoded from that.

06 · Satellite frame

A CubeSat panel board and its tripod tray

The tray slips over a tripod stud. I shortened its corner brackets so they clear the cells, then added hollow ribs so it holds the frame without flexing.
